# Permissions for the proposed household study materials

Research for [socra #28](https://github.com/amolenk/socra/issues/28), checked **24 October 2026**. Planning evidence and uncertainty; no decision on the parent's policy and no blanket legal clearance.

## Findings in brief

1. **Noordhoff's current public General Conditions expressly prohibit AI processing** (article 16.4, p. 4). The current website also says text-and-data mining is not allowed. Neither personal-use wording nor owning a paper book is a documented licence for the proposed Azure processing. Whether these current contractual conditions bind the actual source/user remains unknown. [N1–N4]
2. **Dutch private-copying exceptions provide a real, conditional basis for personal study copies.** Digital photography, image conversion and personal NAS copies are principally an article 16c question; the small-part restriction in article 16b should not simply be applied to every digital copy. Lawful-source, personal-maker/use and noncommercial conditions matter. These provisions do not establish that all copies made by an active external OCR/model service are covered. [L1; C1–C3]
3. **Azure Layout and model inference must be assessed separately from household storage/display and from model training.** Current Microsoft terms prohibit violating others' rights; Microsoft not owning output and not training foundation models on customer data do not grant publisher rights. Layout makes extracted text/structure and temporarily stores inputs/results. The configured model is not yet identified sufficiently to settle all service terms. [M1–M4]
4. **TDM is a conditional exception, not an automatic AI exemption.** The general Dutch/EU exception requires lawful access and no appropriate express rights reservation; Noordhoff's express reservation is directly relevant. The scientific-research exception does not fit an ordinary household study app. Classification of this particular OCR/RAG/inference chain, and the applicability/form of a reservation to the unidentified publication, remain unresolved. [L1; L3; N2, N4]
5. **Outputs differ.** Inspecting lawful originals in a genuinely private household is distinct from public availability; displaying copied regions, quoting, producing summaries, redrawing diagrams and generating similar exercises can each involve reproduction/adaptation. Independent explanations of mathematical ideas and independently drawn mathematical graphs need not copy protected expression. Source references help attribution, but do not themselves authorize copying. [L1–L2; C4]

## 2. Dutch/EU provisions relevant to the actual acts

The official Auteurswet text consulted is **effective 1 January 2026 onward**, accessed 24 October 2026. Article links below use that dated version. EU directives inform interpretation; the Dutch implementation is the starting point for Dutch acts. [L1–L3]

### Reproduction, adaptation and book ownership

- Articles **1, 10, 13 and 14** reserve reproduction/publication of protected works and include whole/partial adaptations or imitation in altered form. OCR is not a rights-free act merely because images become text; image-region copying and source-linked stored text may preserve protected expression. Article **12b** exhausts distribution rights for the particular lawfully marketed physical copy in the EU/EEA, not general reproduction rights. [L1]
- Mathematical concepts, methods and factual relations should be distinguished from original explanatory wording, selection/arrangement, exercise presentation and graphic expression. The Court's originality criterion concerns the author's own intellectual creation; even a short extract can reproduce protected expression. There is no universal safe word count or rule that changing the numbers in an exercise makes the rest original. [C4; L1, arts. 10, 13]

### Personal study copies: 16b versus 16c

**Article 16b(1)** covers a few copies solely for personal exercise/study/use by a natural person, without direct/indirect commercial purpose, including someone ordering copies exclusively for themselves. **16b(2)** limits book copies under that route to a small portion, with specified exceptions such as works for which new paid copies are reasonably not expected. **16b(4)** limits passing the copies to third parties. Crucially, **16b(6) excludes reproductions covered by 16c**. [L1]

**Article 16c(1)** covers reproducing a work or part on a medium intended to play/display/render it, without direct/indirect commercial purpose, solely for the personal exercise/study/use of the natural person making the reproduction. That is the relevant candidate for digital page photos, converted images, NAS/device copies and personal digital text representations. It does **not** repeat 16b's few-copies/small-book-part rule or its express commissioned-copy wording. **16c(2)** assigns the statutory remuneration obligation to the manufacturer/importer of the media; it is not a text imposing an individual textbook-upload fee on the learner. **16c(7)** limits handing the media to third parties; **16c(8)** excludes electronically accessible collections under article 10(3). A photographed ordinary book is not shown to be such an electronic collection; an actual licensed online resource could require a different assessment. [L1]

The **lawful-source condition matters**: the CJEU held that the EU private-copying exception cannot distinguish away unlawful-source copies by covering them in the levy system. A visible copyright footer or having a photograph does not establish lawful provenance, especially for the separate answer page. Neither a stolen/scanned online answer file nor a lawful publisher/school-provided answer book should be assumed. [C1]

**Personal use is narrower than a GDPR-style “household exemption”.** Determine who makes the copies and for whose own study, including parent-operated ingestion and shared LAN display. A household study purpose is important evidence, not an automatic exemption for each reproduction by parent, learner and provider. [L1, arts. 16b–16c; C2–C3]

### Temporary technical copies: article 13a

Article **13a** requires a transient/incidental reproduction, integral and essential to a technical process, whose sole purpose is intermediary network transmission or **lawful use**, with no independent economic significance. EU **2001/29 article 5(1)** has the corresponding test. Persistent NAS originals, OCR/searchable representations, saved summaries and conversation excerpts are not exempt just because they came from a technical pipeline. Service retention for a short time is not by itself enough; the remaining conditions and lawful underlying use still need to hold. [L1–L2; M3]

### Cloud storage is not automatically the same as active OCR/model processing

EU **2001/29 article 5(2)(b)** allows the natural-person/noncommercial private-copying exception with fair compensation. **Austro-Mechana v Strato, C-433/20 (24 March 2022)** applies the concept of reproduction on “any medium” to personal backup copies in cloud storage; cloud servers are not excluded solely by technology. It does not decide this Azure Layout/RAG/summary-generation chain. **VCAST, C-265/16 (29 November 2017)** expressly says natural persons may use third-party copying services (paragraph 35). It then distinguishes a commercial service that also supplies access to protected broadcasts and makes them available to its public; private-copying rules did not authorize that combined service. It is not a categorical judgment that every paid OCR API is unlawful. [L2; C2–C3]

**Current follow-up: Stichting Onderhandelingen Thuiskopievergoeding / Stichting de Thuiskopie v HP Nederland and others, C-496/24 (16 April 2026).** The Court excludes the specified provider-controlled offline streaming copies from the private-copying exception. Paragraphs **42–44** explain that reproduction must be by or on the initiative of a natural person with prior lawful access to, and control of, the source; the provider there held the source and supplied access only after making the copy. Paragraphs **45–48** discuss technical controls that kept the copy within the streaming service. This is important current Dutch-referral authority, but the proposed household has its own page photos and is not described as a publisher-content streaming service. The ruling does not answer whether each Azure OCR/inference reproduction is attributable to the user's initiative and covered by the applicable exception. It does reinforce that entitlement to view controlled digital content is not equivalent to unrestricted copying rights. [C5]

Consequently, **neither “cloud means prohibited” nor “cloud private copying means all AI processing is allowed” is supported**. For this proposal, the individual uploads, service-side input/output copies, extracted searchable text, image crops and returned summaries are distinct acts. The digital-copy exception's personal-maker/user-initiative condition, lawful source/control, provider role, contractual/reservation facts and any applicable alternative exception need to be assessed. The service being paid does not alone establish a commercial purpose for the learner; it does mean the learner and provider's acts should not be conflated. [L1–L3; C2–C3; C5]

### Text-and-data mining (TDM)

- **Auteurswet 25a(3)** / **2019/790 article 2(2)** define TDM as automated analysis of digital text/data to generate information such as patterns, trends and correlations. The definition is not confined to foundation-model training, but neither does the mere use of an AI product prove each operation is TDM. Plain OCR conversion and downstream semantic analysis/inference may raise different classification questions. No reviewed primary judgment conclusively classifies this particular tutoring chain. [L1; L3]
- **Auteurswet 15o(1)–(2)** / **2019/790 article 4** cover TDM reproductions/extractions with lawful access, **unless rights have been appropriately expressly reserved**, and allow retention only as needed for TDM. Recital **18** explains machine-readable reservations for publicly available online content and other appropriate means for other cases. The current website's reservation and General Conditions 16.4 are substantive evidence to consider, not grounds to assume silence/permission. Exactly how a reservation applies to these unknown older/printed/photographed pages is not established. [L1; L3; N2, N4]
- **Auteurswet 15n** / **2019/790 article 3** provide a separate scientific-research TDM exception for qualifying research organisations/cultural heritage institutions with lawful access; it is not the ordinary private study app described here. Article 15n(4) makes that exception noncontractually derogable. **2019/790 article 7** protects articles 3, 5 and 6 from contrary contracts; it does not make general article 4 TDM immune to rights reservations. [L1; L3]
- TDM authorizes specified input reproduction/extraction when its conditions hold; it does not automatically authorize displaying publisher pages or emitting protected-expression adaptations. Each output use still needs its own analysis. A publisher TDM opt-out also does not, by itself, repeal a separate applicable private-copying exception. [L1–L3]

### Household original inspection, excerpts and quotation

Article **12(1)(7)** concerns public on-demand availability; **12(4)** excludes genuinely family/friends/equivalent-circle performances/displays without an access fee from its extended notion of public presentation. The confirmed household-only app does not describe public dissemination. Browser/device copies remain reproduction questions, and being on a LAN is not itself a rights licence or factual access restriction. [L1]

**Article 15a(1)** allows quotation in criticism, review, scientific discussion or comparable-purpose expression where the work was lawfully published, the quotation is socially reasonable and its amount justified by its purpose, moral rights respected and source/author clearly named as reasonably possible. A short passage or graph region directly discussed in an explanation is a candidate; a page viewer or comprehensive extract collection is not converted into quotation simply by adding a citation. No fixed excerpt length automatically satisfies the test. The present unknown title/author/page information limits what accurate attribution can be supplied; it must not be invented. Private copying may be the more relevant route for private original inspection, where its own conditions hold. [L1, arts. 15a, 25; L2, art. 5(3)(d)]

### Educational exceptions are not an automatic household fallback

**Article 16** includes conditional use of portions solely to illustrate teaching, justified by the noncommercial purpose, lawful publication, socially reasonable use, moral rights, attribution and fair remuneration; article **16(5)** concerns digital use under an educational institution's responsibility in a secure environment for its students/staff. **Article 12(5)** has its own defined educational-presentation conditions. **2019/790 article 5** concerns noncommercial teaching under an institution's responsibility, on its premises/other venues or in its secure electronic environment. This privately operated household app is not shown to meet those institutional conditions. School access to a book does not establish that this app inherits the school's rights. [L1; L3; N2, art. 7]

### Generated summaries, derivations, visuals and practice

The input rights question survives even if the output is independently expressed. Conversely, lawful inputs do not make all outputs lawful. A summary that closely preserves original expression/creative arrangement can be a partial adaptation; paraphrasing is not automatic clearance. A source-diagram crop is a reproduction, and a close redraw may be an adaptation. Independently explaining the powers/roots rule, working a mathematical derivation or generating a fresh mathematical graph need not reproduce publisher expression. Similar practice should be assessed for retained wording, diagrams, context and arrangement, not solely whether numeric values differ. These are applications of the reproduction/adaptation/originality rules, not findings about outputs that have not yet been made. [L1, arts. 10, 13–14; C4]

## 5. Exact facts still needed to establish a source-specific conclusion

1. **Publication identity:** title, edition/year, ISBN and relevant copyright/credits/rights notice, as nonprivate bibliographic facts. These establish which source and notices/product terms to compare; they do not themselves establish permissions.
2. **Lawful acquisition/access:** whether the photographed source is an owned, lawfully borrowed/school-issued paper book, authorised digital access or a derived file; who had the entitlement and when. The actual applicable purchase/access/distribution conditions and accepted version, including any express licence, establish contractual scope. Do not assume current public terms retroactively bind every old physical copy or noncontracting family member.
3. **Answer capture:** its source owner, publication and lawful route of access; whether it really belongs to the same publication/entitlement. Matching exercise content does not establish matching rights.
4. **Rights reservation:** the actual work/access-point notice and any incorporated AI/TDM provision, including the appropriate form of reservation for printed versus online material. The current public ban is evidence, not proof that no applicable exception survives or that every unidentified older page carries that reservation.
5. **Actors and operations:** who performs each digital copy and for whose own study; actual household access; exact API/model offer and governing Azure agreement/features. These establish whether the candidate statutory route covers service acts and which provider terms apply.
6. **Output expression and synthetic provenance:** actual extent of quotation/adaptation and author/asset licensing for fixtures, when created. No unmade output can be assessed for substantial expressive similarity now.

The publication/access documents or an existing source-owner licence could establish facts the public research cannot. A specific legal interpretation may still be needed for the active cloud processing/private-copying boundary and the applicable-contract/reservation interaction. **This is not a demand for a publisher approval workflow.** The parent agent can resolve the factual-research ticket with these documented constraints and open facts while leaving the parent's policy choice to the parent.
